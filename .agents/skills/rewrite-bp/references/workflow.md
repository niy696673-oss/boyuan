# Detailed Workflow

## 1. Separate Facts From Story

Extract atomic claims and normalize entities, dates, periods, units, and status.
Resolve or expose inconsistencies before drafting. A rewrite may improve language;
it may not repair weak evidence by making a claim sound more certain.

## 2. Define the Reader Decision

Specify what the reader should understand and what legitimate next step the
material should support. Examples include accepting a meeting, entering an NDA,
authorizing deeper work, or understanding a financing plan. Do not frame the
objective as forcing a positive investment decision.

## 3. Build the Architecture

For each section, record:

- communication objective;
- primary claim;
- supporting proof;
- material qualification;
- expected investor question;
- source IDs and disclosure tier.

Use the architecture to remove repetition and surface missing transitions before
writing polished text.

## 4. Rewrite in Passes

1. `logic_pass`: order claims and expose missing causal links.
2. `evidence_pass`: attach proof and qualifications.
3. `audience_pass`: adjust emphasis for mandate, stage, and role.
4. `language_pass`: improve precision, readability, and Chinese financial usage.
5. `consistency_pass`: reconcile names, figures, periods, and defined terms.
6. `disclosure_pass`: remove information not permitted for the selected tier.

Do not combine editorial and strategy edits in the same approval record.

## 5. Produce Variants

Derive variants from one approved fact ledger. Never maintain parallel fact sets.
Record exactly which sections changed, why, and who approved the change. A blind
teaser, named deck, and detailed memorandum require separate artifact skills.

## 6. Review

Run side-by-side source comparison, numeric tie-out, qualification review,
confidentiality review, and final Chinese-language read. Obtain the required human
approvals before marking a clean copy as externally releasable.
