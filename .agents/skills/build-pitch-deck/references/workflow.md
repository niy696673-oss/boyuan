# Detailed Workflow

## 1. Build the Evidence Map

Normalize approved claims, figures, source locations, periods, units, evidence
states, and disclosure permissions. Identify claims needed for the story but not
yet supported; mark their slides blocked instead of filling gaps.

## 2. Define the Story Spine

Write five short statements before designing slides:

1. what the company enables;
2. why the problem matters now;
3. why this solution can win;
4. what proof reduces the principal risks;
5. what the financing funds and what changes afterward.

Test the sequence against the audience's mandate and likely decision authority.

## 3. Storyboard

Choose slides based on transaction logic. Typical modules include company snapshot,
problem, product, technology proof, market definition, competition, customer proof,
business model, commercialization, operations, team, historical performance,
forecast, round, proceeds, milestones, risks, and appendix.

For each module, select the minimum proof that supports the headline. Move detailed
DD evidence, legal structure, full capitalization, and technical backups to a
controlled appendix when appropriate.

## 4. Specify Visuals

Use charts for quantitative comparisons, process diagrams for causal or operating
flows, tables for exact comparisons, and images only when they prove product or
deployment reality. Avoid decorative visuals that imply unsupported scale.

Every chart must state metric definition, period, unit, actual/forecast status,
and source or calculation ID.

## 5. Build and Render

When native presentation tools are available, generate the deck, render every
slide, and inspect at readable scale. Correct overflow, clipping, font fallback,
misalignment, weak contrast, chart errors, and inconsistent spacing. Repeat until
the rendered artifact matches the approved specification.

## 6. Rehearsal and Release

Prepare likely objections, short answers, evidence references, and items that must
be deferred to NDA or DD. Obtain external-release approval for the final version
and record version, recipient tier, and release date.
