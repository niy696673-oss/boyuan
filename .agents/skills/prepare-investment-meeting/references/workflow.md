# Detailed Workflow

## 1. Diagnose the Meeting, Not Just the Calendar Title

Identify who convened the meeting, what formal gate it supports, what each side
expects, who can decide or sponsor, what prior commitments exist, and what a useful
next step would be. A generic "project discussion" may actually be a screen,
partner review, management DD, term negotiation, or relationship meeting.

## 2. Build the Participant Map

Separate formal title from process role. Look for execution ability, internal
sponsorship, voting or decision authority, information ownership, and relationship
influence. Record evidence and uncertainty. Never convert private speculation into
biographical fact.

## 3. Prepare the Fact Brief

Summarize only decision-relevant facts: company and round, process status, changes
since the last interaction, prior questions, promised materials, unresolved
conflicts, material risks, and requested decisions. Link every figure to its period,
unit, basis, and source.

## 4. Create Hypotheses and Objections

For each likely issue, distinguish known concern, inferred concern, and generic
preparation topic. Write a non-leading test question, likely proof, disconfirming
evidence, and an owner. Do not manufacture certainty about why a fund paused.

## 5. Build the Agenda and Roles

Allocate time based on meeting type and authority. Assign opener, company story,
technical, commercial, financial, terms, questions, timekeeper, and note owner as
needed. Define a fallback agenda for shortened time or missing participants.

## 6. Set Boundaries

Prepare allowed, conditional, NDA-only, and prohibited disclosure lists. Add
landmine handling, escalation phrases, and topics that require counsel, finance,
or technical-owner intervention.

## 7. Plan Capture

Define which decisions, commitments, facts, questions, and action items must be
captured. Agree who validates minutes and what systems may receive update proposals
after the meeting. Do not perform those updates in the preparation workflow.
